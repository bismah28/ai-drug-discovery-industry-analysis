# AI Drug Discovery: Industry Analysis

## Overview

AI is increasingly being used in drug discovery to identify targets, design molecules, and prioritize candidates for testing. This project explores whether those advances are translating into faster drug development, stronger clinical pipelines, and commercial value.

I analyzed 15 selected AI-enabled drug candidates across several biotechnology companies, looking at clinical progress, reported discovery timelines, discontinued programs, and partnerships with major pharmaceutical companies.

## Key Findings

- Selected AI-enabled programs reported early discovery timelines of approximately 9–12 months.
- Of the 10 active candidates in the dataset, 6 remain in Phase I and only 1 has reached Phase III.
- 5 of the 15 selected candidates were discontinued, with 4 progressing beyond Phase I before development stopped.
- Major pharmaceutical companies are already partnering with AI-focused biotechnology firms, showing commercial interest even while late-stage clinical evidence remains limited.
- AI appears most promising for improving the speed and efficiency of early drug discovery, but it is still too early to determine whether it improves overall clinical success.

## Analysis

The project focuses on three questions:

1. Can AI shorten early drug discovery timelines?
2. How far have AI-enabled drug candidates progressed through clinical trials?
3. Is there already a business case for AI drug discovery?

The analysis combines clinical trial records, company pipeline disclosures, regulatory filings, and publicly announced pharmaceutical partnerships.
